- **Chrome 0.2.23: the page shows a taf at work.** While a taf works in a tab, the tab glows round its edges, the bar sits at the top and says "Rae is working here" with Pause and Stop, and a pointer goes to each field or button before the taf acts on it, saying what it does ("Clicking", "Typing", "Choosing", "Ticking", "Pressing a key", "Scrolling", "Reading"), in all 43 languages. The pointer is only drawn: no real mouse moves, and an act lands only on the element it points at; if the pointer can't be drawn, the act is refused. Everything goes the moment the run pauses, stops, is done or the tab is yours again. With reduced motion, nothing moves. The page can't read these signs or change what they say; like anything on a page it can hide them, so the side panel, the tab group and the toolbar are what show a taf is at work, and the side panel's Stop is the one to trust.
