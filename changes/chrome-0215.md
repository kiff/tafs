- **Chrome 0.2.15: a taf in your tab stays inside its sites, and your own moves are yours.** In a tab of yours a taf works in, only its own clicks move it: if you press Back, type an address or reload, your tab is given back to you at once and the taf reads nothing of it, and if your Back leaves the taf's sites, your page loads as you asked. A taf's click or form that would leave its sites is refused before it happens, so your page is never replaced by Chrome's "blocked" page, and a taf never goes Back in your tab. In a taf's own tab, its Back after its own click goes to the page before it (Chrome had skipped it). Closing a taf's tab takes its site fence with it, a restarted extension clears fences left behind, a taf's next page after your tab is given back opens in a new group instead of failing, and a run's first page no longer fails now and then on a busy computer ([docs/chrome.md](../docs/chrome.md)). The end-to-end tests now cover the fence's other ways off a taf's sites, no fence rule left behind (a restarted service worker included), your tab joined by a taf and your own Back in it, and masking in frames ([docs/chrome-e2e.md](../docs/chrome-e2e.md)).
