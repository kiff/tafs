# English (`en`)

**Name:** tafs

**Short description** (113 of 132 characters):

Let your tafs work in your own Chrome, only on the sites you allow, with every risky step approved on your phone.

**Long description:**

tafs for Chrome lets your tafs work in your own Chrome, only on the sites you allow. Every risky step waits for your approval on your phone.

• Pair this Chrome with your phone: in the tafs app, open Account › Chrome › Pair a Chrome and type the code it shows.
• A taf works in its own tabs, in a tab group you can see. A taf you message from the side panel can work in the tab you're on instead, if it's one of its sites, and opens any other page in a tab of its own. In the tab you're on, it can click and type, only on its own sites. You can pause or stop it at any time, and it pauses when you touch one of its tabs. While it works, its tab glows at the edges, a bar at the top names the taf working there and lets you pause or stop it, and a pointer shows each field or button before the taf acts on it. The pointer is only drawn: no real mouse moves. On your other tabs, a small bar lets you pause, stop or go to its tab, and reads nothing from that page.
• Chrome asks once, when you install it, for access to all sites. tafs works only on the sites each task lists, which you approve on your phone.
• A taf stops at a checkout, and before any button it recognises as paying, placing an order or sending money out (a refund, a payout or a transfer). Paying and placing an order then wait for your approval on your phone; sending money out is left for you to do.
• It reads a page as text and form fields, with password, card and code fields removed. It never reads your other tabs or your history.
• A taf can hand you one step on a page, such as typing a search or a card number. You do it yourself, and when it reads the page again, what you typed in the fields is left out.
• tafs also blocks the sites it recognises as banks, wallets, password managers, payment providers (such as PayPal or Stripe), health or government sites, unless you allow one, for one task, on your phone. A taf never opens KIFF's own sites.
• It never keeps your passwords. At a sign-in page, the taf stops and asks you to sign in yourself, in your own tab.
• The side panel shows the taf working in this Chrome, and you talk to it there, as in the tafs app. “Set up a new taf for this site” opens Coach, in its own view. When you send Coach a message, it reads the page in front with the same fields removed, except on sites it recognises as bank, payment, mail or health sites, and sites you switch off, and shows “Reading:” and the site under your message. Only once you allow it, and the same for a message to a taf on one of its sites; you can always send without the page.
• Files you pick go to tafs cloud and Claude, like attachments in the apps.

What a taf reads goes to the AI that runs your task and is kept with that task. KIFF keeps the page before and after a change for at most 30 days. Once screenshots are switched on, each step that acts also keeps one screenshot of the page, taken just before the act with password, card and code fields hidden, for at most 30 days. You see it only in the tafs app, and it never goes to the AI. Uninstalling deletes what the extension keeps on your computer; unpairing keeps only a random id for this Chrome profile and the sites you switched off for Coach.

You need the tafs app on your phone and a KIFF account.
